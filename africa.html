Africa is a land of adventure and wonder — from vast savannas and wildlife safaris to ancient cultures and breathtaking coastlines. Every journey here is filled with unforgettable sights and stories.


EGYPT

Egypt is a timeless land of mystery and wonder offering the iconic Pyramids of Giza, the iconic Nile River and many more. A perfect source to study world’s oldest civilization, hieroglyphs and mummification practices. You can also enjoy desert safari and red sea diving here.

UGANDA

Known as the Pearl of Africa, Uganda has mist filled forests to explore the mountain gorillas, the mighty river, NIle, falling a Murchison falls, and peaceful lake side retreats. The warm locals, colorful wildlife, and spectacular scenery are wild and welcoming.

NIGERIA

Nigeria is a land of energy, whether it is the nightlife that buzzes around Lagos or the colorful festivals or the dramatic scenery such as the Aso Rock and the Yankari National Park. An abundant canvas of music, food and culture beckon travelers to enjoy the rhythm and creativity of Africa to the fullest extent possible.

DR CONGO

Wild and dramatic, DR Congo is a land of great rainforests, erupting volcanoes, and rare wild animals such as endangered mountain gorillas. The Congo River and its spectacular sceneries offer an off-road, untamed adventure that cannot be found on any other part of the continent.

MADAGASCAR

This island nation is a different world, where lemurs are jumping in old baobab trees and beaches with coral which are shining in the sunset. Madagascar has many opportunities to explore and be amazed: Famous rian forest and rocky canyons, colorful markets, and so on.

ALGERIA

Algeria is an impressive mix of Sahara desert, Roman ruins and vibrant cities in the Mediterranean. Explore old caravan paths, climb mountainous terrain, explore old casbahs, and discover the history, culture, and dramatic scenery under endless blue skies.

SOME FEW TIPS:


GET TRAVEL INSURANCE

It’s emphasized as the #1 tip. Insurance can cover medical emergencies, evacuation, lost bags, etc. Especially in remote or remote-access areas.

TAKE NEW US DOLLAR BILLS

Many African countries accept USD; for visas and fees some require very clean, newer bills (post-2009). Old or small-denomination bills may be rejected by money changers.

CARRY AN UNLOCKED SMARTPHONE & GET A LOCAL SIM

Cell coverage and data are surprisingly good in many parts of Africa; buying a local SIM on arrival is often cheap and useful.

USE COMMON SENSE & STAY AWARE OF SAFETY

Don’t wander alone at night in unsafe areas; ask locals or hotel staff about places to avoid; trust instincts. Overall, many risks are avoidable through awareness.

BRING YOUR OWN SUNSCREEN

Sunscreen of good quality can be hard to find locally, or expensive. Better to pack it from home.

PROTECT AGAINST TRAVELER'S DIARRHEA

Don’t assume all food/water is safe. Probiotics, being careful about what you eat, and paying attention to water sources are important.

DRESS DOWN / AVOID FLASHY ITEMS

To avoid unwanted attention, don’t wear flashy jewelry or carry conspicuous luxury items. Dress modestly in terms of style; avoid overly revealing or attention-grabbing clothes.

TAKE A TORCH (FLASHLIGHT), POWER BANK, HEADLAMP

Frequent power outages, especially in rural areas; good lighting and backup power help a lot.

DON'T TAKE WHITE CLOTHING

Dust and dirt are common; light or white clothes soil easily. Pack darker or neutral colours.

ASK PERMISSION BEFORE PHOTOGRAPHING LOCALS

Respect local culture: many people prefer if you ask first, rather than just snapping photos. If they decline, respect that. If they agree, some expect a small tip.

CARRY HAND SANITIZER AND BABY WIPES

For hygiene — after touching surfaces, before eating, or in places where running water / soap is not always available.

BE SMART WITH HUSTLE & VENDORS

You’ll encounter street vendors, people trying to sell or ask for favors. Being polite but firm helps; sometimes behaving like a more worldly visitor (not a “naïve tourist”) reduces hassle.

FIND RELIABLE TAXIS & KEEP CONTACT INFO

If you meet a taxi driver or transport provider you trust, keep their contact details. Good to have someone reliable especially for nighttime or in unfamiliar areas.

PACK A FIRST AID KIT

Bringing a basic medical kit is very helpful; remote areas may lack pharmacies or well-stocked hospitals. Include essentials (bandages, antibiotics, antiseptics, insect repellent, etc.).

MALARIA AWARENESS

Use anti-malarial medications where needed; use insect repellent; avoid mosquito bites (especially at dusk/night), particularly in coastal, lowland, or mosquito-prone areas. Some places at high elevation have lower risk.
